Key findings & root causes
Power subsystem nominal. Solar generation (16.95 W) cleanly exceeds all load modes. Battery recovered from 15 Wh to full in ~1.15 hr. Eclipse-free orbit at β ≈ 78° is physically consistent with inclination 97.6°, RAAN 60°, epoch 2000-03-01. No power anomalies found.
Orbit propagation consistent. 14 revolutions, 96.49 min period, and a near-constant beta angle across 24 h all check out against analytical estimates. Orekit numerical propagator appears configured correctly.
Wheel momentum exceeded datasheet limit once. Peak recorded momentum is 0.0173 Nms vs the config-declared max of 0.01317 Nms. No saturation event was flagged, suggesting the saturation-check threshold may be inconsistent with the physical limit — worth auditing before CDR.
Science duty cycle lower than expected (40% of SAA time). DETECTION mode ran for only 0.30 hr out of 0.74 hr of SAA dwell, and data volume (0.067 MB) is ~43% of what the rate equation predicts. The most likely cause is payload pointing validity failures (avg error 16.47° vs the 15° threshold) gating science generation off. Consider relaxing the threshold or improving SAA attitude guidance.
SAA pass count low for 14 orbits. Six SAA passes out of 14 orbits (~43%) is on the low end. At 97.6° inclination and 600 km, the SAA polygon should be crossed on most equatorial descending passes. Verify the Shapely SAA polygon definition covers the full anomaly extent — a clipped or under-sized polygon would cause this undercounting.
Zero downlink despite two ground contacts — root cause: relay pointing error. The average relay pointing error (72.64°) is more than 7× the relay_pointing_valid threshold (10°). The warning at T+0.70 hr ("relay pointing error exceeded 90 deg") confirms the antenna is not being steered toward the ground station during RELAY mode. The relay link activation gate (gs_off_boresight_deg ≤ 40°) is never satisfied. This is the single biggest mission failure in the simulation and must be resolved before downlink can function.
Large slews and the 113° max slew are worth reviewing. Four slews exceeded 60°. The largest (113.23°) suggests a nearly reversed attitude target. At 5°/s max rate and 1°/s² acceleration, a 113° slew takes ~25–30 s — short but energetically non-trivial. Confirm these are valid mode transitions and not guidance calculation artifacts.
